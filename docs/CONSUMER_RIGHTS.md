# Consumer Rights & Checkout Compliance

EU market policy can define withdrawal period, legal guarantee period, default delivery expectations, the previous-price lookback period and checkout presentation rules. Defaults are market data rather than hard-coded business logic so national rules can override them.

Discounts use durable price history. The EU baseline uses the lowest price applied during at least the previous 30 days as the reference for an announced price reduction. Price history is market, currency, customer-group and variant aware.

Checkout presents the main characteristics, final payable price including unavoidable charges and delivery before the purchase action. Optional paid extras are never preselected. The final action label must clearly communicate the obligation to pay.

Digital content can require separate explicit consent to start performance immediately and acknowledgement that starting performance may remove the withdrawal right where the applicable rule allows it. This acknowledgement is stored in the immutable checkout snapshot rather than inferred from a generic Terms checkbox.

The order stores the legal-policy versions and consumer-policy snapshot effective at purchase time. Later edits to Terms, returns or warranty documents do not rewrite historical orders.
